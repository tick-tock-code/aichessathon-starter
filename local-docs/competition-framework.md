# Competition framework

## 1. Competition format

AI Chessathon is an AI chess engineering competition. It begins with an online qualification phase from 4–11 September 2026. Selected participants will be invited to an in-person final at Encode Club, London on 12 September 2026. The match format, clocks, compute and network limits, submission rules, and the qualification and selection process are set out in the [documentation](https://aichessathon.com/docs). Teams are limited to three members.

Registration and the online qualification are open worldwide. The final qualification stage and the in-person final are for teams where every member is a UK university student; finalist teams are verified before invites are confirmed. Participants must provide accurate registration information. An invitation to the final is personal to the selected participant or team and may not be transferred without organiser approval.

## 2. Agents and submissions

Participants must build and operate their own chess agent within the published interface, compute, network, and time limits. Permitted open-source libraries, models, external services, hardware, and collaboration rules are set out in the [documentation](https://aichessathon.com/docs). Participants must disclose components and assistance when the specification requires it.

Agents must produce legal moves, operate reliably, and must not attempt to access another participant’s system, hidden match data, organiser infrastructure, or credentials.

## 3. Fair play and enforcement

No participant may manipulate pairings, coordinate match outcomes, impersonate another entrant, exploit infrastructure outside the intended game interface, or misrepresent authorship or eligibility. Suspected vulnerabilities should be reported privately to hello@aichessathon.com and not used for advantage.

Organisers may pause a match, request logs or a reproducible build, rerun a match affected by a verified technical problem, or disqualify a submission for a material breach. Decisions will be based on the published rules, available evidence, and consistent treatment of participants. An appeal process will accompany the final technical rules.

## 4. Code of conduct

Participants must treat other participants, organisers, venue staff, and online communities with respect. Harassment, discrimination, threats, deliberate disruption, doxxing, or retaliation are not permitted. Communicate constructively, respect personal boundaries, and follow reasonable safety and venue instructions.

Report conduct concerns to hello@aichessathon.com. Reports will be handled as privately as reasonably possible and shared only with people who need to respond.

## 5. Intellectual property and event data

Participants retain ownership of their original agent code. Entry grants the organisers only the limited rights needed to run, test, review, and record the competition. Any separate request to publish code, demonstrations, participant profiles, photography, or recordings will be explained before use.

Match results, pairings, rulings, and factual event records may be published. Private registration data and CVs are handled under the privacy notice.

## 6. Updates and precedence

This page is the competition framework; the operational detail lives in the [documentation](https://aichessathon.com/docs). Material changes will be versioned and communicated to registered participants, who will be asked to acknowledge any change that materially affects participation. If documents conflict, the latest dated competition rules take precedence for competition matters.

Questions can be sent to hello@aichessathon.com.
